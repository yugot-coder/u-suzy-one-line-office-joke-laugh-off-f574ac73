# Candidates — One-Line Office Joke Laugh-Off

Competition: `u-suzy-one-line-office-joke-laugh-off-f574ac73`
Gate: UTF-8 text, 1–200 characters, not only white space, one line.
Judge (in order): (1) really a one-line office joke, (2) sharp, surprising
punchline, (3) clear on one read, (4) fresh — avoid office clichés,
(5) tight wording, every word earning its place.

Deliberately avoided clichés: "per my last email", "this meeting could have
been an email", "reply all", "circle back", "synergy", "TGIF / case of the
Mondays", "working hard or hardly working", "you're on mute".

## The 10 candidates

1. (101 chars) "There's a yoghurt in the shared fridge from 2019 — it's now technically our longest-serving employee."
   — **CHOSEN.** Specific, visual, instantly clear. The setup is a universal
   office horror (the ancient thing nobody dares bin in the shared fridge);
   the punchline reframes it as an HR fact. Fresh angle, twist lands on the
   last word, no cliché anywhere.
2. (96 chars) "I wrote 'as discussed' in an email about something we never discussed, and everyone just nodded."
   — Sharp observation about bluff culture; the nodding is the punchline.
   Slightly more "knowing chuckle" than laugh-out-loud.
3. (92 chars) "The office thermostat has two settings: Arctic research station and sauna with a dress code."
   — Good imagery, but the punchline is a comparison rather than a twist.
4. (88 chars) "My out-of-office reply says I'll respond when I return — I've been back for three weeks."
   — Clean irony, but the joke is mostly the premise itself; punchline is mild.
5. (99 chars) "I asked IT for a second monitor; they gave me a mirror and said, 'Now you can watch yourself work.'"
   — Fun twist, but the quoted dialogue costs words and the setup is long.
6. (88 chars) "Our standup is the only meeting where everyone stands up and nobody stands for anything."
   — Neat wordplay on "stand", but "standup meeting" jokes are well-trodden.
7. (85 chars) "My calendar is so full that my lunch break now needs its own invite and a Teams link."
   — Relatable and tight, but the observation is familiar; no real surprise.
8. (110 chars) "The boss said 'think outside the box', so I moved my desk outside — now I'm the only one with a window office."
   — Literal-interpretation twist works, but it is the longest line and the
   "think outside the box" setup is itself a cliché.
9. (97 chars) "I expense my coffee as client entertainment because the client is me, and I am very entertaining."
   — Self-aware and funny, but the punchline repeats the setup's word
   ("entertainment/entertaining") instead of surprising.
10. (87 chars) "The Wi-Fi password is 'password123' and the security training takes forty-five minutes."
    — Good irony, but reads more as an observation than a joke with a punchline.

## Why #1 wins

- **Office joke, unambiguously:** shared fridge + longest-serving employee is pure office life.
- **Punchline:** "longest-serving employee" reframes a rotting yoghurt as a colleague with tenure — a sharp, surprising promotion.
- **One read:** no jargon, no setup to explain; the picture forms instantly.
- **Fresh:** no email/meeting/mute clichés; the fridge-yoghurt angle is specific, not generic.
- **Tight:** 101 characters, every word earns its place; the em dash is the only pause, and the twist is the final word.
